# Transfers to manual accounts are cash flow, not transfers

A manual account (tracking `manual`: nothing imported, e.g. a joint savings account or a loan) may have an IBAN. Payments between an imported account and that IBAN are recognised as **transfers to a manual account** (user's decision, 2026-11-18, proposals 8451f12f and d6a9b6e4: "laina = lainanhoito, säästö = säästäminen, ei kulutusta").

We considered treating them like linked transfers, which the Ledger leaves out of income, expense and net. We decided against it: a linked transfer moves money between two accounts the app sees, so leaving both sides out keeps every total right, but a transfer to a manual account has only one side, and the money really left the imported accounts. Excluding it would make the net, the monthly overview, the runway and the forecast look better than the cash they describe. So such a payment stays a countable transaction (cash flow) and carries `ownTransfer` with a kind from the manual account's type: `saving` (savings, investment) or `loan_repayment` (loan, other liability), which spending analyses (insights, the briefing's one-off and category findings) leave out and the summary and monthly overview show apart; `other` (manual checking, card, property, other asset) is reported like any payment, because what the money bought there is not seen.

Recognition is derived on every read from the account's IBAN, not stored as links. Setting, changing or removing an IBAN therefore re-evaluates every stored transaction and every later import with no migration or backfill, and undoing the account change set restores the earlier reporting exactly; the change set lists the transactions whose reporting changes, which is the audit trail. Import pairing is unchanged: if the import links a payment to a manual account's IBAN as one side of a pair (e.g. a card repayment to an issuer IBAN the user also registered), the link wins and the payment stays a transfer. Counterparty names are never used as an identifier: the same person is often paid on several accounts, and a name match would silently move real spending out of view.
